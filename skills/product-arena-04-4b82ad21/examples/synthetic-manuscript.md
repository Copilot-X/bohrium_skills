# Defect-Engineered NiCo Catalysts for Low-Temperature CO2 Methanation

## Abstract

We compared NiCo-200 and NiCo-50 for CO2 methanation at 200–300 °C and 1 bar.
NiCo-200 reached 85% CH4 selectivity at 250 °C, versus 65% for NiCo-50.
The result suggests that defect enrichment suppresses CO formation.

## 1 Introduction

CO2 methanation can store renewable hydrogen in the existing gas grid.
Ni-based catalysts are active at low temperature but often produce CO as a
by-product. Defect engineering has been proposed to tune CH4 selectivity,
yet comparisons are rarely made at a fixed space velocity. Here we compare
NiCo-200 and NiCo-50 at 200–300 °C and 1 bar under the same space velocity.

## 2 Results

NiCo-200 reached 85% CH4 selectivity at 250 °C, compared with 65% for
NiCo-50. Both samples were stable for 12 h on stream. Selectivity decreased
above 275 °C for NiCo-200. These observations indicate that defect
enrichment is associated with higher CH4 selectivity, although the
mechanism remains uncertain.

## 3 Discussion

The higher selectivity of NiCo-200 is consistent with stronger CO
adsorption on defect sites. However, the defect concentration was not
measured operando, so a causal claim is not justified.
