# Synthetic example: oxygen vacancies and selectivity

> Synthetic example only. This is not a formal case answer.

## Abstract

Surface oxygen vacancies are proposed to regulate the CO2 hydrogenation selectivity
of CeO2. We tested three samples at 300–400 °C and 1 bar. The vacancy-rich sample
reached 92% methanol selectivity at 350 °C, compared with 78% for the pristine
sample. The results suggest that oxygen vacancies may stabilize the formate
intermediate, but the operando evidence remains indirect [1].

## 1 Methods

The catalyst was calcined at 500 °C for 4 h and reduced in H2 at 400 °C for 2 h.
Each condition was measured three times. Selectivity was calculated from gas
chromatography peak areas at a test pressure of 1 bar.

## 2 Results

The vacancy-rich sample reached 18% conversion and 92% selectivity at 350 °C
(Table 1). Selectivity decreased above 375 °C, while CO formation increased.
Fig. 1 shows the selectivity–temperature curves.

Table 1. Catalytic performance at 350 °C and 1 bar.

| Catalyst | Conversion (%) | Selectivity (%) |
|---|---|---|
| CeO2 | 12 | 78 |
| CeO2−x | 18 | 92 |
